POST-OPERATIVE INSTRUCTIONS — LAPAROSCOPIC CHOLECYSTECTOMY

Patient: [REDACTED] | Date of Surgery: [DATE]
Surgeon: [REDACTED] | Hospital: [REDACTED]

PROCEDURE:
Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (keyhole removal of the gallbladder) performed under general anaesthesia. Procedure was uncomplicated. You had [NUMBER] small incisions in your abdomen.

EXPECTED RECOVERY:
Most patients return to light activities within 1–2 weeks and full activity by 4–6 weeks. You may feel some shoulder tip pain for a day or two from the gas used during surgery — this is normal and usually resolves without treatment.

WOUND CARE:
- Keep wound dressings dry for 48 hours.
- Dissolvable sutures do not need removal.
- Do not submerge wounds in a bath or swim for 2 weeks.
- Shower with clean water — pat dry gently.

PAIN MANAGEMENT:
- Paracetamol 1g up to four times daily as needed.
- Ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily with food if paracetamol alone is insufficient (avoid if you have kidney problems or stomach ulcers).
- Do NOT take more than the recommended doses.

DIET:
Resume a normal diet gradually. A low-fat diet is recommended for the first 4–6 weeks while your body adjusts to the absence of a gallbladder.

ACTIVITY:
- No heavy lifting (>5 kg) for 4 weeks.
- No driving for at least 48 hours after general anaesthesia; do not drive until you can perform an emergency stop comfortably.
- Light walking is encouraged from day 1.

RETURN TO EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT IF:
- Fever above 38°C
- Yellowing of skin or eyes (jaundice)
- Severe worsening abdominal pain
- Heavy bleeding from wound sites
- Inability to keep fluids down

FOLLOW-UP:
GP review at 2 weeks for wound check (or earlier if concerns).
