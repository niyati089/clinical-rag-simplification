CANCER TREATMENT — ENDOCRINE THERAPY AND RADIOTHERAPY REFERENCE

HORMONE RECEPTOR-POSITIVE BREAST CANCER — ENDOCRINE THERAPY

TAMOXIFEN
- Class: Selective Oestrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM)
- Mechanism: Competitively inhibits oestrogen binding to ER in breast tissue
- Indication: Adjuvant therapy for ER+/PR+ breast cancer (pre- and post-menopausal women; also used in men)
- Standard dose: 20 mg once daily orally
- Duration: 5 years (standard); 10 years if high risk of recurrence
- CRITICAL: Do NOT stop Tamoxifen without discussing with oncologist — early discontinuation significantly increases recurrence risk

Side effects and their significance:
- Hot flushes, night sweats (vasomotor symptoms) — common; manage with lifestyle measures; pharmacological options available if severe
- Vaginal dryness or discharge — report any abnormal or unusual vaginal bleeding promptly (risk of endometrial changes)
- Thromboembolism risk (DVT/PE): Tamoxifen increases the risk. Report sudden leg swelling, calf pain, shortness of breath, or chest pain immediately
- Endometrial changes: Annual gynaecological review; report any post-menopausal bleeding urgently
- Mood changes: Document and report to oncologist if severe
- Teratogenicity: Avoid pregnancy during treatment and for 2 months after stopping. Use reliable non-hormonal contraception

Drug interactions:
- Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., Paroxetine, Fluoxetine) significantly reduce Tamoxifen efficacy — use alternative antidepressants
- Warfarin: Tamoxifen can enhance anticoagulant effect; monitor INR closely

AROMATASE INHIBITORS (Anastrozole, Letrozole, Exemestane)
- Indication: Post-menopausal ER+ breast cancer (or after ovarian suppression in pre-menopausal women)
- Mechanism: Inhibit aromatase enzyme → reduce peripheral oestrogen synthesis
- Side effects: Musculoskeletal pain (arthralgia/myalgia), bone density loss → monitor with DEXA scan; osteoporosis risk
- Bone protection: Calcium and Vitamin D supplementation; consider bisphosphonate

ADJUVANT RADIOTHERAPY — BREAST CANCER

Indications:
- After breast-conserving surgery (wide local excision): whole-breast radiotherapy reduces local recurrence risk by approximately 50%
- After mastectomy in high-risk patients (positive lymph nodes, large tumour)

Standard schedules:
- Hypofractionated: 15–16 fractions (3 weeks) — standard for most patients; equivalent efficacy to longer courses
- Conventional: 25 fractions (5 weeks)

Side effects (expected):
- Skin: erythema (redness), desquamation, fatigue — usually temporary; resolves within weeks of completing treatment
- Fatigue: common during and after treatment; usually improves within 4–6 weeks

Long-term risks (less common):
- Lymphoedema of the arm (especially after axillary treatment)
- Radiation-induced cardiac toxicity (minimised with modern techniques — breath-hold, prone positioning)
- Secondary malignancy risk (very small)

Skin care during radiotherapy:
- Wash area gently with mild, unscented soap
- Avoid tight clothing over treated area
- Avoid sun exposure during and for 1 year after treatment
- Apply aqueous cream or recommended moisturiser to affected skin (not immediately before radiation)
