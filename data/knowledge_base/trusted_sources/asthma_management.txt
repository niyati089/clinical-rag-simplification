ASTHMA MANAGEMENT — PATIENT AND CLINICIAN REFERENCE

DEFINITION
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways characterised by variable and recurring symptoms of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing, particularly at night or early morning.

DIAGNOSIS
- Characteristic symptoms (wheeze, cough, dyspnoea, chest tightness)
- Variable expiratory airflow limitation on spirometry (FEV1/FVC ratio <0.7, with reversibility after bronchodilator)
- FeNO (fractional exhaled nitric oxide) >40 ppb supports eosinophilic airway inflammation

STEP-WISE PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT (ADULTS)

Step 1 — Mild Intermittent:
- Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA), e.g., Salbutamol 100 mcg — 2 puffs as required

Step 2 — Regular Preventer:
- Add inhaled corticosteroid (ICS), e.g., Beclometasone 200–400 mcg/day in divided doses
- ICS reduces airway inflammation; must be used EVERY DAY even when well

Step 3 — Add-on Therapy:
- Add long-acting beta-2 agonist (LABA), e.g., Salmeterol or Formoterol
- Or increase ICS dose
- Or add leukotriene receptor antagonist (LTRA), e.g., Montelukast

Step 4 — Specialist referral:
- Consider biologic agents (e.g., Mepolizumab for severe eosinophilic asthma)

INHALER TECHNIQUE — IMPORTANT
- Incorrect inhaler technique is one of the most common causes of poor asthma control
- Spacer devices should be used with pressurised metered-dose inhalers (pMDI) for children and elderly patients
- Mouth should be rinsed after ICS inhalers to prevent oral candidiasis (thrush)

MONITORING AND CONTROL
- Use reliever inhaler (SABA) more than 3 times/week = inadequately controlled asthma
- Check: symptom frequency, night waking, exercise limitation, reliever use, peak flow
- Review inhaler technique at every appointment

ASTHMA ACTION PLAN
Every patient should have a written personal asthma action plan:
- Green zone: No symptoms — continue preventer
- Yellow zone: Symptoms increasing — use reliever, double preventer dose (if advised)
- Red zone: Severe attack — use reliever every 20 minutes, call emergency services

ACUTE SEVERE ASTHMA — EMERGENCY CRITERIA
Seek emergency care immediately if:
- Unable to complete sentences in one breath
- Respiratory rate >25/min
- Peak flow <50% best
- Oxygen saturation <92%
- Signs of severe distress, cyanosis, silent chest (very dangerous — reduced air movement)

COMMON TRIGGERS
- Allergens: house dust mites, pet dander, pollen, mould
- Respiratory infections (especially viral)
- Exercise
- Cold air
- Cigarette smoke (active and passive)
- NSAIDs and aspirin (in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease)
- Occupational exposures (isocyanates, flour dust)

PAEDIATRIC CONSIDERATIONS
- Children under 12 should use spacer with pMDI
- Tidal breathing technique with spacer for children under 5
- Spacer and face mask for infants
- Rinse mouth after preventer inhalers
- Ensure school has an asthma management plan and a spare reliever inhaler
