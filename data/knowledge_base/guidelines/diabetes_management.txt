DIABETES MELLITUS — TYPE 2 MANAGEMENT REFERENCE

DIAGNOSIS
Diabetes is diagnosed by:
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L on two occasions
- HbA1c ≥ 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) on two occasions
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L with symptoms

GLYCAEMIC TARGETS
- HbA1c target: generally <53 mmol/mol (7.0%) for most patients on glucose-lowering medication
- Individualise targets based on age, comorbidities, hypoglycaemia risk, and patient preference

LIFESTYLE
- Dietary modification: reduce refined carbohydrates, increase fibre, reduce saturated fat
- Regular physical activity: ≥150 minutes/week of moderate intensity
- Weight management: even 5–10% weight loss significantly improves glycaemic control

PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT
First-line: Metformin (if tolerated and eGFR ≥30 mL/min)
- Start at 500 mg daily with meals, increase gradually to minimise GI side effects
- Maximum dose: 2000 mg/day (in divided doses)
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30), hepatic failure, and acute illness with risk of dehydration

Second-line options:
- SGLT-2 inhibitors (Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin): recommended with cardiovascular or renal disease; reduce HbA1c ~0.5-1%, also protect kidneys and heart
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (Semaglutide, Liraglutide): significant weight loss benefit; beneficial in cardiovascular disease
- DPP-4 inhibitors (Sitagliptin, Alogliptin): weight-neutral, well tolerated
- Sulfonylureas (Gliclazide): low cost, but hypoglycaemia risk
- Insulin: added when HbA1c remains above target on oral agents

SGLT-2 INHIBITOR SAFETY
- Risk of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — may occur even with near-normal blood glucose (euglycaemic DKA)
- Sick day rules: stop SGLT-2 inhibitor if: vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, unable to eat, or before surgery
- Genital mycotic infections are more common

MONITORING
- HbA1c: every 3–6 months until stable, then every 6 months
- Renal function (eGFR): at least annually; more frequently with declining function or SGLT-2 inhibitor/Metformin use
- Blood pressure: at every diabetes review
- Foot examination: annually or more frequently in high-risk patients
- Eye screening (retinal photography): annually
- Cholesterol: annually

HYPOGLYCAEMIA
- Symptoms: sweating, shaking, palpitations, confusion, weakness
- Treatment: 15–20 g of fast-acting glucose (glucose tablets, fruit juice)
- If unconscious or unable to swallow: glucagon injection (if available) or call emergency services

DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS (DKA) — EMERGENCY
- Symptoms: excessive thirst, frequent urination, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity-smelling breath, confusion
- DKA is a medical emergency — call emergency services immediately
