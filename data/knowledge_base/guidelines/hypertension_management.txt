HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT — CLINICAL GUIDELINE SUMMARY

DEFINITION AND CLASSIFICATION
Hypertension is defined as a persistent elevated blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg in clinic settings (≥ 135/85 mmHg on ambulatory monitoring).

Classification:
- Stage 1: Clinic BP ≥ 140/90 mmHg; ABPM daytime ≥ 135/85 mmHg
- Stage 2: Clinic BP ≥ 160/100 mmHg; ABPM daytime ≥ 150/95 mmHg
- Severe: Clinic systolic ≥ 180 mmHg or diastolic ≥ 110 mmHg

TREATMENT TARGETS
- General population under 80 years: <140/90 mmHg in clinic
- Patients with diabetes or CKD: <130/80 mmHg
- Age ≥ 80 years: systolic 130–150 mmHg if tolerated

LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS
Recommended for all patients:
- Reduce dietary sodium: target <6 g/day (less than 1 teaspoon of salt)
- DASH diet: high in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy
- Regular aerobic exercise: ≥30 minutes, ≥5 days/week
- Reduce alcohol consumption to ≤14 units/week for women, ≤21 for men
- Smoking cessation
- Weight reduction in overweight/obese patients (each 1 kg weight loss reduces systolic BP by ~1 mmHg)

PHARMACOLOGICAL THERAPY — STEP-BY-STEP
Step 1: ACE inhibitor (e.g., Ramipril) or ARB (e.g., Losartan) for patients with diabetes or CKD
         OR calcium channel blocker (e.g., Amlodipine) for all others
Step 2: Combine CCB + ACE inhibitor/ARB
Step 3: Add thiazide-like diuretic (e.g., Indapamide)
Step 4: Consider adding spironolactone (low-dose) or alpha/beta-blocker

MONITORING
- Blood pressure: at every GP visit and via home monitoring (2 readings, morning and evening, for 7 days)
- Renal function and electrolytes: annually; more frequently in CKD or when adding/changing diuretics or RAS blockers
- Potassium: especially important with ACE inhibitors/ARBs and potassium-sparing diuretics

DRUGS TO AVOID
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac): raise blood pressure and reduce efficacy of antihypertensives; worsen renal function, especially in CKD
- Licorice root supplements: raise blood pressure
- Combined oral contraceptives: may elevate blood pressure; discuss alternatives

HYPERTENSIVE URGENCY/EMERGENCY
- Severe hypertension (≥180/110 mmHg) with signs of end-organ damage (chest pain, neurological symptoms, visual disturbance) is a medical emergency — call emergency services immediately.
