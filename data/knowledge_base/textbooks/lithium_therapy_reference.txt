LITHIUM THERAPY — CLINICAL REFERENCE

INDICATIONS
- Bipolar affective disorder (mania, depression, maintenance)
- Augmentation of antidepressants in treatment-resistant depression
- Prophylaxis of recurrent unipolar depression

MECHANISM
Lithium's exact mechanism is not fully understood. It is believed to modulate intracellular signalling pathways, including inositol phosphate and protein kinase C pathways, and may enhance serotonergic transmission.

DOSING AND THERAPEUTIC MONITORING
- Lithium has a narrow therapeutic index — small changes in dose or fluid balance can cause toxicity
- Therapeutic serum levels:
  * Acute mania: 0.8–1.0 mmol/L
  * Maintenance (long-term prophylaxis): 0.6–0.8 mmol/L
  * Older adults: 0.4–0.7 mmol/L (lower due to reduced renal clearance)
- Blood sampling: 12 hours after last dose (trough level)
- Frequency: Weekly until stable, then every 3 months
- Concomitant monitoring: Renal function (eGFR, creatinine), thyroid function (TSH), calcium — every 6 months

FACTORS THAT RAISE LITHIUM LEVELS (and risk of toxicity):
- Dehydration (diarrhoea, vomiting, fever, heat, exercise without adequate fluid intake)
- Low-sodium diet (sodium depletion causes lithium retention by kidneys)
- Drug interactions (see below)
- Renal impairment

DRUG INTERACTIONS
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac): Reduce renal lithium clearance → raise levels → toxicity risk
- ACE inhibitors and ARBs: Increase lithium levels; monitor closely when starting/adjusting
- Diuretics (thiazides): Reduce lithium excretion → toxicity; loop diuretics generally safer
- Haloperidol and other antipsychotics: Increased neurotoxicity risk at higher levels

LITHIUM TOXICITY
Mild (1.5–2.0 mmol/L):
- Coarse tremor, nausea, diarrhoea, drowsiness, muscle weakness

Moderate (2.0–2.5 mmol/L):
- Confusion, slurred speech, ataxia (unsteady gait), prolonged QT on ECG

Severe (>2.5 mmol/L) — MEDICAL EMERGENCY:
- Seizures, coma, cardiac arrhythmias, renal failure, permanent neurological damage possible

Management of toxicity:
- Stop lithium
- IV hydration with normal saline
- Haemodialysis for severe toxicity
- Urgent toxicology/nephrology consultation

PATIENT COUNSELLING
- Take lithium at the same time each day (usually night)
- Drink adequate fluids (1.5–2 L/day in normal conditions; more in hot weather or exercise)
- Avoid drastic changes to salt intake
- Carry a lithium alert card
- Report symptoms of toxicity immediately: coarse tremor, confusion, slurred speech, unsteady walking
- Sick day rules: If vomiting or unable to keep fluids down, seek medical advice; may need dose withholding and IV fluids

EFFECTS ON THYROID AND KIDNEYS
- Hypothyroidism: Lithium can cause thyroid enlargement and hypothyroidism (15–40% incidence with long-term use); monitor TSH regularly
- Renal: Long-term use can cause nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (polyuria) and, rarely, chronic kidney disease; monitor eGFR

TERATOGENICITY
- Lithium is associated with cardiac malformations (Ebstein's anomaly) in the first trimester
- Discuss pregnancy planning with psychiatry team; risk-benefit must be carefully assessed
- If continued in pregnancy, switch to multiple smaller doses and monitor levels more frequently
