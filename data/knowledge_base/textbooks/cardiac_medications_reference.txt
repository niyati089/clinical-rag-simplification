CARDIAC MEDICATIONS — PATIENT REFERENCE GUIDE

ANTIPLATELET THERAPY

Aspirin
- Mechanism: Inhibits platelet aggregation by blocking COX-1 irreversibly
- Common doses: 75–100 mg daily (long-term secondary prevention); 300 mg loading dose (acute ACS)
- Indications: Secondary prevention after myocardial infarction, stroke, PCI; stable coronary artery disease
- Side effects: GI irritation, increased bleeding risk
- Take with or after food to reduce stomach irritation
- Do NOT stop abruptly without medical advice after cardiac events

Ticagrelor (Brilique)
- Mechanism: Reversible P2Y12 inhibitor; faster onset than clopidogrel
- Dose: 90 mg twice daily (for 12 months post-ACS); 60 mg twice daily (maintenance beyond 12 months)
- Indication: Acute coronary syndrome (STEMI, NSTEMI), post-PCI
- CRITICAL: Do NOT stop without consulting a cardiologist — risk of life-threatening stent thrombosis
- Avoid: Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin); aspirin doses >100 mg/day
- Side effects: Dyspnoea (common, usually mild), bleeding, bradycardia

STATINS (CHOLESTEROL-LOWERING)

Atorvastatin
- High-intensity statin
- Dose: 40–80 mg once daily (usually at night)
- Indication: Primary/secondary prevention of cardiovascular events; post-ACS (80 mg)
- Side effects: Myopathy (muscle pain), rarely rhabdomyolysis; liver enzyme elevation
- Report: Unexplained muscle pain or weakness — rare risk of serious muscle breakdown

BETA-BLOCKERS

Metoprolol Succinate
- Cardioselective beta-1 blocker
- Dose: 25–200 mg once daily (extended release)
- Indications: Heart failure (reduced ejection fraction), post-MI, angina, hypertension
- Do NOT stop abruptly — may precipitate rebound angina or arrhythmia; taper dose
- Caution: Asthma, COPD (relative contraindication); peripheral vascular disease

ACE INHIBITORS

Ramipril
- Mechanism: Inhibits ACE → reduces angiotensin II → vasodilation + reduced aldosterone
- Common doses: 1.25–10 mg once or twice daily
- Indications: Hypertension, heart failure, post-MI cardioprotection, diabetic nephropathy
- Common side effect: Persistent dry cough (switch to ARB if intolerable)
- Serious warning: Angioedema (swelling of lips, tongue, throat) — STOP immediately and seek emergency care
- Monitor: Renal function and potassium (especially when starting or dose-adjusting)
- Avoid in pregnancy (teratogenic)

PROTON PUMP INHIBITORS (for GI protection)

Pantoprazole
- Dose: 20–40 mg once daily (before breakfast)
- Indication: Reduce GI bleeding risk, especially with dual antiplatelet therapy
- Generally well tolerated; long-term use may reduce magnesium and vitamin B12 absorption
